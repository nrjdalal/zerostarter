The public `/waitlist` page takes an address and reports an approximate count, rounded down in steps of five once it passes a threshold. That is all a visitor sees, deliberately: an exact number tells a competitor how you are doing, and tells an early signup they are one of four.

**Console > Waitlist > Signups** is the other half, and the only place the addresses themselves are readable. It is `admin` and above, like every console page that shows an address.

## When you need this

Reach for this page when you are launching before the product and want somewhere to collect addresses, or when signups have arrived and you need to reach, count or remove them. The surface is the `waitlist` [feature](/docs/manage/features), and the [CLI](/docs/getting-started/cli#init) lists its default in a new fork; for who reaches the console at all, see [Allowlist](/docs/manage/allowlist).

## What you can do with it

In the order you reach for them, finding signups first and acting on them after:

- **Search** by address, and sort by address or by when they signed up. Newest first by default, because the question is usually who arrived since you last looked.
- **Copy** a selection. The addresses come back one per line, oldest first, because a copy here exists to be pasted into whatever sends the mail. Not JSON: field names would only be in the way, the opposite of [Activity](/docs/manage/activity), whose rows go into a ticket where the field names carry the meaning.

  A selection covers **the rows that have loaded**, not the whole list. The table loads as you scroll, so select-all after two batches selects fifty signups out of however many there are. That is fine for reaching this week's signups and wrong for a mailout: for the whole list, page through `GET /api/v1/admin/waitlist` rather than scrolling the table until it ends.

- **Remove** a signup, one row or a selection. It is deleted, they can sign up again, and the removal is recorded in Activity, because the row is gone afterwards and the record is the only place the address survives.

<Callout type="warn" title="Removing does not erase the address">

The row leaves `waitlist`, and `activity` keeps the sentence that names it: `Removed ada@example.com from the waitlist`. Removing an allowlist rule leaves the same kind of line, `Removed @example.com from the allowlist`, and there that is deliberate, since an admin authored the rule; it is a different thing for a signup a member of the public typed into a public form. Someone asking to be taken off your list is asking to be forgotten, and by default this moves their address rather than deleting it.

Nothing prunes `activity` either, so decide before you launch. Two ways out, both small: word the summary without the address (`waitlistRemoveSummary` in `packages/db/src/console.ts` is the only place it is written), or delete the matching `activity` rows in the same transaction as the signup.

</Callout>

There is no way to add a signup from the console, and no `POST` on the admin route. The list is what the public form collected; typing an address in by hand would make it something else.

## The one console table that can get big

Every other console list is the size of your staff. A waitlist is the one that plausibly holds six figures, and the console's Signups page is built like the others: search is `ILIKE '%term%'`, which cannot use the unique index on the address, and paging is by offset, so a deep page counts past every row before it. The one that bites first is neither: every batch runs its own `count(*)` beside the rows, because the table shows a total and paging needs one, so scrolling ten batches counts the whole table ten times.

That is fine for thousands and slow for hundreds of thousands. Three independent fixes when your own numbers ask for them, and not before: a `pg_trgm` GIN index on `waitlist.email`, keyset paging instead of the offset, and counting only on the first page. See [Database](/docs/manage/database).

## The flag takes the whole surface

`waitlist` in `packages/config/src/site.ts` governs the public page, its API and the console surface together; what each does while it is off, and when a fork's home picks up the change, is on [Features](/docs/manage/features). The table survives: dropping it would be a migration, and a fork that turns the waitlist back on should find its signups where it left them.

## Common questions

**Why is the waitlist its own nav group rather than a third item under Access?**

Access is Users and Allowlist: who may reach the console and what they may do there. Nobody on a waitlist has access to anything, so filing it there would say something untrue. Same rung, `admin`, because the page shows addresses.

**Why does the form accept every address, even one already on the list?**

`POST /api/waitlist` lowercases the address and inserts with `onConflictDoNothing`, so the response never reveals whether an address is already there, and a hidden `subject` field is a honeypot: a bot that fills it is answered `ok` and stored nowhere. The one refusal is a malformed address, a `400` with `VALIDATION_ERROR`.

**Is the signup form the form pattern to copy?**

Copy the Forms convention in the `design` skill instead. The signup form is self-contained and works, on the same `@tanstack/react-form` and zod stack, but its one inline field skips the `FieldGroup` the convention puts around every form, so it is an instance, not the template.

## It's working if

- `/waitlist` renders the form with no count badge; the badge appears at the tenth signup and reads `10+`, then `15+`, in steps of five.
- A submission with the hidden `subject` field filled answers `{ "data": { "message": "ok" } }` and no row appears in Console > Waitlist > Signups.
- With `waitlist: false`, `/waitlist` returns 404 and the console nav has no Waitlist group.
